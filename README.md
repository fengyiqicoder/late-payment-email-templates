## How to use them

1. Find the row that matches the client's last reply, or "No reply" if there wasn't one.
2. Pick the tone for how late it is. Friendly is for the first reminder, firm is for two to three weeks overdue, and final is for when you're ready to act.
3. Replace every `{placeholder}`. Keep the invoice number, amount and original due date in every email so the thread stands on its own.
4. Send it as a reply in the same thread, so the history stays attached.

## A few rules that make them work

- **One ask per email.** Ask for a payment date or a name, not both plus an apology.
- **Turn promises into dates.** "Next week" becomes "by Friday, November 27", confirmed in writing.
- **Only threaten what you'll do.** Pausing work, adding the late fee in your contract and filing in small claims court are all real options. Pick one before you write the final email.
- **Late fees need a contract.** You can usually only charge one if your agreement or accepted invoice terms say so, and many states cap them. To see what a late fee and the delay cost you, use the [late payment calculator](https://followup.onetagai.com/late-fee-calculator/).
